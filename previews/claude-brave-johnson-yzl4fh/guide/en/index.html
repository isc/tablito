The principles

Tablito is not just a quiz. Every design choice is grounded in research from cognitive psychology and mathematics education. Five pillars hold the app together:

  • Spaced repetition — the Leitner boxes. Each fact lives in one of five boxes numbered from 1 (just learned) to 5 (firmly anchored). A new fact starts in box 1: it is reviewed the same day. A correct answer moves it up one box and pushes the next review further out: 1 day in box 2, 3 days in box 3, 7 days in box 4, 21 days in box 5. A mistake sends it back to box 1, long enough to re-anchor it. The child reviews each fact just before forgetting it, with longer and longer intervals — far more durable than cramming in a single evening. Kang (2016) ; Cepeda et al. (2008) ; Rea & Modigliani (1985)
  • Low interference. Facts that look alike (same operand, close answers) are never introduced in the same week. A session contains only facts dissimilar enough that the child won't confuse them in memory. Dotan & Zviran-Ginat (2022)
  • Interleaving. The tables are mixed within a single session rather than worked through one after another. The child has to reach for the right operation at every question, which solidifies long-term recall. Rohrer & Taylor (2007) ; Rohrer, Dedrick & Burgess (2014)
  • Understand before memorizing. Each new fact is first shown as a grid of dots (repeated addition), then through commutativity (3 × 5 = 5 × 3), and finally with a derivation trick suited to it (× 9 = × 10 − n, × 4 = double-double, × 6 = × 5 + n, etc.). A few anchor facts (doubles, × 5, × 9, squares) support the derived ones. The scaffolding fades away once recall becomes automatic. Van de Walle via Wichita Public Schools (2014) ; Brendefur et al. (2015)
  • Progress-oriented feedback, not performance. No numeric score on the child's side, no stars computed from a success rate: only steady encouragement and a spotlight on the facts learned. The goal is intrinsic motivation and mastery, not the grade. The raw numbers stay available in the parent area. Butler (1988) ; Hattie & Timperley (2007)

Welcome

On the very first launch, a four-step welcome:

  • The mascot introduces itself.
  • The child's name.
  • An introduction to the placement test.
  • The test: 15 well-spread questions.

Facts already known start straight in the higher Leitner boxes.

Home screen

Where every day starts.

  • The mascot welcomes the child, cheers correct answers, encourages after a mistake. It never judges.
  • The flame counts days in a row.
  • The big button starts the day's session.
  • The bottom bar opens the mystery picture, the badges and the ×1 / ×10 rules.
  • The gear opens the parent area, behind a small multiplication.

Once all the tables are mastered, the day's session moves on to division by itself: no new button.

The session

12 to 15 questions, a few minutes.

  • A new fact is first shown in three steps: a grid of dots (repeated addition), swapping the factors (3×5 = 5×3), then a trick (“× 9 is × 10 minus one”).
  • Answers with the keypad or by voice. A button below the keypad switches mode, and the choice is remembered.
  • Quick correct answer: a golden star.
  • Mistake: the right answer, the grid of dots, the trick if the fact is recent. The question comes back a little later.

Session recap

At the end of every session:

  • The day's new facts, if any.
  • Progress: a “X facts known out of 36” bar.
  • The mystery picture to go and see, with a mention when it has changed.
  • Confetti for a mastered table, a completed picture or a new badge.

My mystery picture

An 8×8 grid (tables 2 to 9): each cell is a piece of a hidden picture.

  • The better a fact is known, the sharper its cell: blurry silhouette, flat color, colors, shadows, details. One step per Leitner box.
  • A forgotten fact blurs a little again, with no notion of failure.
  • All 36 facts mastered: the picture is fully revealed.

At the top, the discovered / mastered / total counts.

Level 2 — division

Once all the tables are mastered (the “Times tables genius” badge), the same facts come back the other way round: “56 ÷ 7 = ?”.

  • The trick is taught: for 56 ÷ 7, look for “7 times what makes 56?”.
  • Step by step: divisions arrive in the same order as the tables, easiest first.
  • One session: it switches to division, and slips in a few tables to maintain.
  • A new mystery picture. “My picture” becomes “My pictures”; the tables picture stays earned.

Everything else (Leitner boxes, encouragement) works as for the tables.

Level 3 — division with remainders

Once every division is mastered, division that doesn't come out even: “45 ÷ 7 = 6 r 3”, the skill expected at the end of grade 3.

  • The trick: find the multiple just below, without going over. What's missing is the remainder.
  • Two steps: “How many times?”, then “What's left over?”.
  • Never the same sum: the number to divide changes on every review. Sometimes the remainder is zero: up to the child to spot it.
  • A third mystery picture. Tables and divisions keep being maintained in the same session.

The badges

Badges to mark the way.

  • Milestones: first session, 7 days, 30 days.
  • Performance: 10 correct answers in a row, 5 answers under 2 s.
  • Mastery: one badge per table, then “Times tables genius”. Division adds its own.

Each badge opens on its condition and, while still locked, a progress bar.

The ×1 and ×10 rules

Two rules given up front, so they don't have to be learned by heart:

  • × 1: the number doesn't change.
  • × 10: the digits slide one place to the left, a 0 takes the ones place.
  • × 11, a bonus once tables 2 to 9 are mastered: repeat the digit (3 × 11 = 33). A “New” dot flags it.

These tables are therefore not among the 36 facts to learn.

Parent area

Open it from the gear on the home screen, after a small multiplication that checks an adult is behind the screen. From top to bottom:

  • Today. Has the session been done? The last 14 days, sessions, streaks.
  • The week. Days practised, accuracy, speed, facts that moved up, compared with the week before.
  • One card per subject. The current level and its mastery bar. Tap it for the subject's page: boxes, Leitner grid, trends, session history.
  • Needs practice. The three toughest facts right now, with an idea to help at home.
  • Settings. Remote follow, profiles and backup, daily reminder, language, help.

A question, a problem, an idea? The “Send feedback” button, top right.

Several children

One tablet for the whole family: each child has their own profile, progress, badges, streak and pictures.

  • Add a child: from the parent area (“Profiles and backup”) or the “Who's playing?” screen.
  • With two or more profiles, the app asks “Who's playing?” on launch, and a button at the top of the home screen switches player. With one, nothing changes.
  • In the parent area, one chip per child, including those followed remotely, without switching player.
  • “Profiles and backup”: move to another device, export or import a backup, delete a profile.
